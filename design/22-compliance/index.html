    Design: Compliance mapping and the evidence pack¶

    Status: Accepted (spec, v0.13.0). Implementation follows in a separate PR. Created: 2026-10-14 Depends on: 17-actors.md (the actor and kardinal audit export), 18-actor-policy.md (party rules, freezes), 19-verdicts.md (Verdicts), and the v0.13 specs 20-attestations.md and 21-receipts.md (in review when this was written; the rows that rely on them are marked planned). Verified against: this repository at 417c943 (main). Control texts are paraphrased from the standards. A row marked "verify against the standard" is one where the clause numbering, not the control's substance, must be checked before the user page ships.

    1. Problem¶

    An auditor asks a team running kardinal two things: "show me that every production change was authorised, reviewed by someone other than its author, and recorded", and "show me the records". kardinal already keeps those records in the cluster: AuditEvents, Approvals, gate instances with their overrides, Bundles with their verified creator and provenance, and from v0.12 the actor stamp, AgentIdentities and freezes. But nothing says which record answers which control, and nothing collects the records for a period into one package an auditor can keep.

    This spec defines:

    • the control mapping: for each control in scope, the exact kardinal record that evidences it, what the customer still has to do, and whether kardinal ships it today;
    • kardinal audit evidence-pack: one command that writes every record of a period, with a manifest of hashes and the mapping, as a single archive;
    • the user page that publishes the mapping, limited to what is shipped.

    kardinal is not a GRC tool and does not certify anything. It produces evidence for the change part of the controls; the customer's policies, reviews and other systems cover the rest.

    2. Where the evidence lives today¶

    Record Kind / field What it proves Written by Kept for

    Record Kind / field Spec

    From v0.13 (planned, specs in review): signed PromotionReceipt per promotion per environment (21-receipts), verified attestations and commit signatures on ImageVerification (20-attestations).

    Two facts shape the evidence pack:

    • Records are deleted. AuditEvents by retention, Bundles by historyLimit (and their Approvals and gate instances with them, through owner references). The cluster is a working store, not an archive. The AuditEvent is the record meant to outlive the Bundle.
    • Records are mutable by cluster admins. Admission pins who wrote them and the CRDs freeze them after creation, but someone with delete on auditevents can remove one. Tamper evidence for a period needs either a copy taken out of the cluster in time (SIEM export) or signed receipts (21-receipts).

    3. The control mapping¶

    Status: shipped (on main), v0.12 (accepted spec, in review), planned (v0.13 spec). "Customer" is what kardinal cannot do for you.

    3.1 SOC 2 — CC8.1 (change management)¶

    Requirement: changes to infrastructure and software are authorised, designed, tested, approved and implemented under control; points of focus include tracking changes, approving them, segregating the duties of developers and approvers, and handling emergency changes.

    Point of focus kardinal record Status
    Changes are tracked AuditEvent promotion actions per Bundle and environment; Bundle provenance shipped
    Changes are approved before production approval gates (spec.approval), Approval objects, status.approvals, ApprovalRecorded; pr-review environments with the kardinal/gates status shipped
    Developer and approver are different people approval.excludeAuthor against the admission-pinned kardinal.io/created-by; for agents, the one-party rule shipped; agents: v0.12
    Emergency changes are controlled GateOverridden (who, why, capped expiry), freeze exemptions FreezeExempted shipped; freezes: v0.12
    Changes are tested before release health checks and verification (PromotionStep Verified), MetricChecks, upstream soak gates; Verdicts shipped; Verdicts: v0.12
    Evidence is tamper-evident signed receipts planned

    Customer: the change policy itself, the review of the evidence, changes that bypass kardinal (direct git pushes: protect the branches, require kardinal/gates), access reviews of who holds the approver role.

    3.2 ISO/IEC 27001:2022 — Annex A 8.32 (change management)¶

    Requirement: changes to information processing facilities and information systems are subject to change management procedures.

    Element kardinal record Status
    The procedure is enforced, not advisory Pipeline + PolicyGates: a change reaches an environment only through a PromotionStep the gates allow shipped
    Planned, approved, recorded as CC8.1 above shipped
    Rollback RollbackStarted/RollbackSucceeded, holds shipped
    Change windows ChangeWindow blackout/recurring; freezes shipped; freezes: v0.12

    Customer: the documented procedure, the risk assessment of a change, communication to affected parties.

    3.3 ISO/IEC 27001:2022 — Annex A 5.3 (segregation of duties)¶

    Requirement: conflicting duties and areas of responsibility are segregated.

    Element kardinal record Status
    Author is not approver excludeAuthor; status.approvals[].reason names "the version's author" shipped
    An agent and the person accountable for it count as one the one-party rule; Approval.spec.actor; AgentIdentity.spec.sponsor v0.12
    Distinct roles the chart's user roles (<release>-viewer, -promoter, -approver, -admin); approvers by allowedUsers/allowedGroups shipped
    Quorum approval.required: N distinct parties shipped (parties: v0.12)

    Customer: who is granted which role, and the access review.

    3.4 NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5¶

    Control Requirement (paraphrase) kardinal record Status
    CM-3 Configuration change control determine the types of controlled changes; review and approve them with their security impact in mind; document decisions; implement approved changes; retain records; monitor and review change activity the Pipeline and gates define controlled changes; approvals and gate results document the decision; PromotionStep implements it; AuditEvents retain it; kardinal audit summary / the evidence pack review it shipped (record retention: as configured)
    CM-3(2) Testing, validation and documentation test changes before implementing them health checks, MetricChecks, soak gates, Verdicts shipped; Verdicts v0.12
    CM-5 Access restrictions for change define, approve and enforce access restrictions for changes Kubernetes RBAC with the chart roles; virtual subresources (pipelines/pause, policygates/override, bundles/reject); <release>-scoped-writes, -graph-objects policies; AgentIdentity scope shipped; agent scope v0.12
    CM-5(1) Automated access enforcement and audit records enforce access restrictions automatically and keep audit records of enforcement ValidatingAdmissionPolicies (deny, not warn); APIWrite AuditEvents for UI and Bundle API writes, refusals included shipped; APIWrite v0.12
    AC-5 Separation of duties identify duties needing separation and define access to support it excludeAuthor, one-party rule, user roles shipped; agents v0.12

    Customer: the configuration management plan, the change control board's charter, the system's other components.

    3.5 DORA — Regulation (EU) 2022/2554¶

    The request named Article 17. In the Regulation, Article 17 is the ICT-related incident management process (detect, manage, record and notify incidents), not change management. Change management is in Article 9(4)(e) (documented policies, procedures and controls for ICT change management), detailed in the RTS on the ICT risk management framework (Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2024/1774), whose article on ICT change management is, to our reading, its Article 17 (verify against the standard). Both are mapped:

    Provision Requirement (paraphrase) kardinal record Status
    Reg. Art. 9(4)(e); RTS 2024/1774 change management changes are recorded, tested, assessed and approved in a controlled way, with emergency changes handled and documented as CC8.1 and CM-3 shipped
    Reg. Art. 17 incident management incidents are detected, logged, classified and followed up not kardinal's job. kardinal contributes: the change record an incident investigation needs (what changed where, by whom, when), the rollback record, the stop-the-line freeze with who froze and why partly: rollback shipped, freezes v0.12

    Customer: incident classification and reporting to the authority (Art. 18–19), the ICT risk management framework.

    3.6 EU AI Act — Regulation (EU) 2024/1689¶

    These articles bind providers and deployers of the AI systems the Act names (Art. 12 and 14: high-risk systems; Art. 50: systems that interact with people or generate content). A coding agent that changes software is usually none of these. kardinal does not make anyone compliant with the AI Act; it gives an organisation that runs agents the records these articles ask of AI systems, applied to the agents' changes. The user page says so in these words.

    Article Requirement (paraphrase) kardinal record Status
    Art. 12 Record-keeping high-risk AI systems allow automatic recording of events (logs) over their lifetime, to identify risks and support monitoring every change an agent makes is an AuditEvent with actor.kind: agent, its sponsor and asserted model/session; kardinal audit export v0.12
    Art. 14 Human oversight people can oversee, understand, override and stop the system ("stop" button, Art. 14(4)(e)) agents: approve (a person approves), agent autonomy, suspension of an AgentIdentity, kardinal freeze --agents, stop-the-line v0.12
    Art. 50 Transparency people are told when they interact with AI; AI-generated content is marked in a machine-readable way the actor stamp marks each agent-made version and decision machine-readably (spec.actor.kind: agent), shown in the PR evidence and UI; signed commit attribution v0.12; commit signatures planned (20-attestations)

    Customer: deciding whether the Act applies to its systems, its own AI system documentation.

    3.7 ISO/IEC 42001:2023 (AI management system)¶

    Clause and Annex A numbering below must be verified against the standard before the user page ships.

    Area (Annex A, verify numbering) kardinal record Status
    A.3.2 AI roles and responsibilities AgentIdentity.spec.sponsor (an accountable person per agent), scope v0.12
    A.6.2.8 recording of event logs of AI systems agent AuditEvents with actor v0.12
    A.9 use of AI systems (responsible use, human oversight in operation) autonomy per environment, opt-in agent approvals, freezes, suspension v0.12

    Customer: the AI management system itself: policy, risk and impact assessments, objectives.

    4. kardinal audit evidence-pack¶

    kardinal audit evidence-pack --since 2026-07-01 [--until 2026-10-01] [-A | -n <ns>]
    [--pipeline <name>] [--environment <env>] [-o pack.tar.gz]

    4.1 What it contains¶

    evidence-pack/
  manifest.json            # what, when, who exported, filters, per-file sha256, counts, coverage
  controls.json            # the mapping of section 3, each control -> files and record kinds
  auditevents.ndjson       # kardinal audit export --format json, the period
  auditevents.ocsf.ndjson  # the same, OCSF 1.3.0 (17-actors §7)
  approvals.ndjson         # Approvals that still exist
  policygates.ndjson       # gate instances of the period: spec (expression, approval, overrides) and status
  bundles.ndjson           # Bundles: actor, created-by, provenance, rejection, phase, environments
  pipelines.ndjson         # the Pipelines (the procedure: environments, approval modes, agents ceilings)
  policygate-templates.ndjson  # the gate templates in force at export (the policy)
  changewindows.ndjson     # windows and freezes, with exemptions                       (freezes: v0.12)
  agentidentities.ndjson   # the agent registry                                          (v0.12)
  verdicts.ndjson          # Verdicts of the period                                       (v0.12)
  receipts.ndjson          # PromotionReceipts, with their signed envelopes               (planned)
  admission.yaml           # the identity ValidatingAdmissionPolicies and bindings in force

    Kinds the cluster does not serve (an older chart) are skipped and listed in the manifest's coverage.missing, so a pack never claims evidence it lacks.

    4.2 Format¶

    Format For Against
    (a) A directory Easy to browse Not one artefact to hand over or hash; a partial copy looks complete
    (b) One .tar.gz with a manifest One file, streamable, standard tools; the manifest says what is in it Needs extraction to browse
    (c) A .zip Opens by double-click on every desktop Random-access format: Go's writer must seek, or keep the directory in memory; no streaming to stdout
    (d) An OCI artifact pushed to a registry Content-addressed, signable with cosign Needs a registry and push rights; auditors rarely use one

    Choice: (b), written to a file or stdout. The manifest records each file's SHA-256 and the pack's own digest is printed at the end, so the exporter can note it in the ticket.

    4.3 Integrity¶

    Mechanism For Against
    (a) SHA-256 per file in the manifest Detects corruption and edits after export Proves nothing about who produced it: whoever edits a file can rewrite the manifest
    (b) The CLI signs the manifest (sigstore-go, keyless or a key) Origin and integrity of the pack The CLI runs with the caller's kubeconfig. It cannot use the controller's ServiceAccount identity (keyless) or read its key Secret without granting auditors the signing key, so the signature would be the exporter's anyway. It also cannot prove records were not deleted before export.
    (b') The controller signs the pack's manifest digest on request The controller's identity, as for receipts A new API surface that signs whatever digest a caller sends: a signing oracle, unless it rebuilds the pack itself
    (b'') The exporter signs the pack with cosign (cosign sign-blob, or cosign attest-blob with the manifest as predicate), under their own identity No signing code or key in kardinal. The signature names who exported, which is what an auditor asks. The exporter's key custody and policy apply. One more step for the exporter; the user page gives the command
    (c) Rely on per-promotion signed receipts (21-receipts) inside the pack Each promotion's record was signed by the controller when it happened; deletion of a record leaves a gap a receipt chain can show Only promotions, not every AuditEvent; planned

    Choice: (a) and (c), with signing left to the exporter (b''). - kardinal audit evidence-pack has no --sign. It writes the per-file SHA-256 manifest (a) and, with 21-receipts, receipts.ndjson: each promotion's record as the controller signed it when it happened (c). kardinal verify-receipt --file checks those offline. - The pack itself is signed by whoever exports it, with cosign. The user page gives cosign sign-blob --bundle pack.tar.gz.sigstore.json pack.tar.gz (keyless or --key) and the matching cosign verify-blob. - (b) and (b') are rejected. A CLI signature could only be the exporter's identity, which cosign already gives without kardinal holding signing code. A controller-side signer for packs would sign digests it did not compute.

    The spec is honest about (a): it is a checksum, not a signature, and the user page says so.

    4.4 What period the cluster can still prove¶

    Approach For Against
    (a) Export what the cluster holds; report coverage No new dependency Retention and historyLimit may have deleted records of the period
    (b) Also read an external archive (a bucket the export wrote to before) Complete history A second storage format and credentials in the CLI
    (c) Require a SIEM and only export from it The SIEM is the archive of record kardinal cannot read every SIEM

    Choice: (a), with coverage stated, not assumed: manifest.coverage gives, per kind, the oldest record found, and the pack warns that retention (--audit-retention-max-age, --audit-retention-max-per-pipeline) and Pipeline historyLimit may have deleted records inside the period. A period older than the oldest AuditEvent fails with an error that says to export continuously with kardinal audit export into the SIEM. The user page recommends a daily kardinal audit export --since 25h into the SIEM as the archive of record.

    4.5 Mapping file¶

    Where the mapping lives For Against
    (a) The docs page only Simple The pack does not say what it evidences
    (b) A Go table (pkg/compliance) that writes controls.json and generates the docs page's tables One source; the pack and the page cannot disagree; a test checks that every record kind named exists in the scheme A small package
    (c) OSCAL assessment-results The NIST machine format GRC tools import Large schema; few kardinal users have an OSCAL pipeline

    Choice: (b); OSCAL output is a follow-up once someone asks for it.

    4.6 Scale and memory¶

    Each kind is read with paginated List calls (500 per page), written as NDJSON to a temporary file per kind while its SHA-256 is computed, then appended to the tar stream (a tar entry needs its size first). Memory is bounded by one page; disk by the largest kind. Gate instances are selected by their Bundle's creation time inside the period, through the kardinal.io/bundle label.

    4.7 RBAC¶

    The CLI runs with the caller's kubeconfig: read (get, list) on the kinds above, in the namespaces asked for, plus get on the admission policies (cluster-scoped). The chart adds a <release>-auditor ClusterRole with exactly those reads and no write, extending the read-only role in docs/guides/security.md#rbac-read-only-access-to-audit-records. Kinds the caller may not read are listed in coverage.forbidden, not silently dropped.

    5. The user page¶

    docs/compliance.md, under the Security section of the nav, published with the evidence-pack command:

    • the tables of section 3, generated from pkg/compliance, listing only shipped rows; a row whose feature is not released is left out, not marked "coming";
    • what kardinal does not cover, per control (the Customer lines);
    • how to archive (kardinal audit export into a SIEM) and how to produce a pack;
    • the honest limits of section 6.

    The agentic section links it from "What kardinal enforces today".

    6. What this does not do¶

    • It does not certify. It maps records to controls; an auditor judges them.
    • It cannot prove a record was not deleted by a cluster admin before export, until signed receipts (21-receipts) give each promotion a signed record outside the cluster.
    • Changes that bypass kardinal (a direct push to the GitOps branch, kubectl apply on the workload) are invisible to it. Protect the branches and require the kardinal/gates status.
    • The AI Act rows are analogies unless the customer's agent is itself a system the Act covers; the page says so.

    7. Decisions¶

    # Question Options Choice and why
    C1 Pack format directory; tar.gz; zip; OCI artifact tar.gz + manifest (4.2): one streamable file, standard tools
    C2 Integrity hashes; signed manifest; signed receipts Hashes, and the controller-signed receipts inside the pack; the exporter signs the pack with cosign (4.3). Stated as a checksum, not a signature.
    C3 Period coverage cluster only, with coverage; external archive; SIEM only Cluster, with coverage reported (4.4); SIEM export recommended as the archive
    C4 Mapping source docs only; Go table; OSCAL Go table (4.5), generating both controls.json and the page
    C5 DORA "Art. 17" map Art. 17 as change management; map Art. 9(4)(e) + RTS only; map both, labelled Both, labelled (3.5): Art. 17 is incident management in the Regulation
    C6 AI Act framing claim compliance; omit; map as analogous records with the applicability caveat Analogous, with the caveat (3.6)
    C7 Unshipped rows on the user page show as "coming"; omit; show greyed Omit (5): the docs say only what is shipped

    8. Test plan¶

    • Unit: pkg/compliance table: every record kind and AuditEvent action it names exists in the API scheme and the AuditActions list; every control has a Customer line. The page's tables are generated and a test fails when they are stale (as the CLI reference is).
    • CLI: evidence-pack against a fake client: filters, pagination across pages, manifest hashes match the files, coverage reports a kind the scheme lacks and a forbidden kind, a period older than the oldest AuditEvent fails with the export advice, deterministic order.
    • Live e2e (core, COUNT=2, coverage rows): a promotion with an approval and an override, then a pack: every record of the promotion is in it, hashes verify with sha256sum -c, the user page's cosign sign-blob and verify-blob commands work on the pack (key mode), the receipts in it verify with kardinal verify-receipt --file offline, and a user bound to <release>-auditor only can produce it.
    • Scale: 100,000 AuditEvents exported with memory bounded by one page (the scale suite's profile records the CLI's peak RSS).
