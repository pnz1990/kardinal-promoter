    Signed promotion receipts¶

    v0.13, off by default

    Receipts are new in v0.13 and off until you set receipts.enabled. Enabling them creates receipts for promotions that reach Verified from then on; earlier promotions get none.

    A promotion receipt is a signed record of one promotion. It says what version was promoted to which environment, who made it and for whom, which gates it passed, who approved it, what the health check said, and which pull request carried it. The controller signs it with Sigstore and keeps it in the cluster. It can also push it next to the image in your registry and log it in Rekor, so anyone can check it without access to the cluster.

    $ kardinal verify-receipt checkout prod --key receipt.pub --require HEALTHY,!OVERRIDDEN
receipt checkout-1-5-0-prod: every check below that was made passed
  ✓ signature:        key sha256:6f1e9c2a4b8d0e37
  – transparency log: no entry
  ✓ statement:        the cluster's spec.statement, sha256:9d3a...
  – subject digest:   not pinned (give --image or --subject)
  ✓ resource:         kardinal://1f0c.../namespaces/payments/pipelines/checkout/environments/prod (namespace, pipeline, environment pinned)
  ✓ levels:           HEALTHY, !OVERRIDDEN as required
  ✓ bundle:           checkout-1-5-0 is the Bundle the receipt was made for (UID 1b2c...)
record:
  environment: prod (pipeline checkout)
  version:     checkout-1-5-0 (1.5.0)
  actor:       agent fixer for alice@example.com; asserted: model claude-opus-5
  signed by:   key
  change:      https://github.com/acme/deploy/pull/412
  gate checkout-1-5-0-prod-approve: pass (approved: 1 of 1 (version 1.5.0))
  approved by: carol@example.com
  levels:      KARDINAL_GATES_PASSED, KARDINAL_APPROVED, KARDINAL_HEALTHY
  policy:      sha256:4be1...

    The design, with the options weighed for each choice, is docs/design/21-receipts.md.

    What a receipt records¶

    kardinal creates one receipt per PromotionStep that reaches Verified: a promotion or a rollback. A step that failed or was superseded changed nothing and gets none; its AuditEvents record it.

    The receipt is an in-toto statement with a SLSA verification summary predicate (https://slsa.dev/verification_summary/v1). Tools that read verification summaries (Kyverno, the sigstore policy-controller, slsa-verifier) read its core. kardinal adds the full record in the extension field kardinalPromotion, which those tools ignore.

    Part Content
    subject Each image digest (sha256), and the config commit (gitCommit) of a config or mixed version. A version with neither (images by tag only) is named by its Bundle spec's digest.
    resourceUri kardinal://<cluster>/namespaces/<ns>/pipelines/<pipeline>/environments/<env>. The cluster is the kube-system Namespace UID (or receipts.clusterID), so two clusters' prod never match.
    policy The Pipeline generation, and a digest of the policy set: the Pipeline's spec hash and each gate template's UID, generation and spec digest, as they were when the version's Graph was built (the gate instance carries them, kardinal.io/gate-template-{uid,generation,digest}): a template edited later is not what the gate evaluated, so the receipt does not name it. A template's status writes do not change it.
    verificationResult Always PASSED: a receipt exists only for a promotion that landed.
    verifiedLevels What the record shows, and nothing more: KARDINAL_GATES_PASSED (every required gate passed), KARDINAL_APPROVED (at least one counted approval), KARDINAL_VERIFIED_BY_AGENT (a verify gate counted a passing Verdict), KARDINAL_HEALTHY (the health check passed), KARDINAL_OVERRIDDEN (a gate passed by override), KARDINAL_FREEZE_EXEMPTED (the promotion went on through a freeze under an exemption), and SLSA_BUILD_LEVEL_<n> (the version's SLSA build level, 1 to 3: see kardinalPromotion.checks). A verifier usually requires the overridden and freeze-exempted levels absent: --require GATES_PASSED,!OVERRIDDEN,!FREEZE_EXEMPTED.
    kardinalPromotion.version The Bundle: name, UID, version, type, images, config commit, provenance.
    kardinalPromotion.actor Who made the version and for whom (the actor); asserted fields stay under asserted.
    kardinalPromotion.gates Each required gate as it was when the step started: expression, result, reason, when it was evaluated, the override in effect (with its actor and whether it was verified), its approvals (with their structured fields) and, for a verify gate, its Verdicts, with whether each counted.
    kardinalPromotion.changeRequests Each change request ticket of the version in the environment, as it was when the step started: template, instance, provider, ticket key and URL, state and the provider's state, whether the ticket is the promotion's (verified, and verifiedReason), approvers, timedOut, and when it was read (observedAt) and until when that reading counted (validUntil). Left out when there is none.
    kardinalPromotion.checks The per-promotion MetricCheck results, the ImageVerification result per image, and the health check. checks.slsa is the version's SLSA build level from its newest Verified ImageVerification (attestations) when its policy decides one (slsa): recorded image by image (checks.slsa.images): an image with a verified attestation that decides a level has status: verified and its buildLevel (the highest of those), and every other image status: none, since an attestation that did not verify never counts. The images are every digest-pinned image of the Bundle (each is a subject of the receipt, and a VSA's verifiedLevels hold for every subject), including one the Pipeline's imageVerification.images does not select: that one is never checked, so it is none and the version claims no level. A subject that is not an image, a mixed or config version's config commit, is listed in checks.slsa.otherSubjects as none: no SLSA build attestation kardinal verifies covers a commit, so such a version claims no level either. The version's buildLevel is the lowest of its images', as attestations.<name>.slsa.buildLevel folds them for a gate, and 0 when an image is none. levelSource (builder or vsa) is kept only when every image's level was decided the same way. A level of 1 or more is claimed as SLSA_BUILD_LEVEL_<n>, so a version with an unchecked image never claims one.
    kardinalPromotion.change The environment and the PR (URL, number, merge commit), or the commit pushed for approval: auto. For a PR the step adopted after a restart rather than opened (PR evidence), pr.adopted holds its head commit (headSHA), the SCM account that opened it (openedBy), and each commit kardinal did not push with its author (unpushedCommits, at most 20; unpushedCount is how many there are). Names have no e-mail address, bidi or zero-width characters and are at most 128 bytes; a commit ID that is not hex is (invalid). A receipt of a PR kardinal opened has no adopted key and is byte for byte what it was before.
    kardinalPromotion.rollbackOf The version a rollback restores.
    kardinalPromotion.freezeExemptions Each freeze the promotion went on through under an exemption (kardinal override --freeze): the freeze and whom it held, who made the exemption (with their actor stamp), why, its environment or version when it named one, and until when it counted. The step records them as it goes on (status.freezeExemptions). They cover the receipt's own step: a step deleted and created again (a retry) starts a new record. A step let through by v0.12, which did not record them, has freezeExemptionsUnknown: true instead (when the freeze is still on at its next git step, the record is written then), and its receipt claims KARDINAL_FREEZE_EXEMPTED all the same, so --require '!FREEZE_EXEMPTED' refuses it.

    Gates are recorded when the step starts¶

    When a step leaves Pending, it records the gates it just checked, and its change request tickets, in status.decision, in the same write that moves it to Promoting. The receipt uses that record, not the gates at Verified. A schedule gate that passed at 10:00 and reads false at 10:20, when the health check ends, is recorded as passed: that is what the promotion started on.

    The record is bounded (128 KiB): it leaves out what a receipt never shows (an approval's comment, a Verdict's summary), and a step with many heavy gates keeps the approvals and Verdicts that counted, cuts long texts to 1 KiB and drops the change requests' approvers (counted in approversOmitted), and then leaves out the last gates (omittedGates); the receipt says so (truncated: true, the gates left out in omittedGateNames, and truncated on each gate it cut), and its policy digest still covers every gate. The receipt's levels are decided over every gate before any cut: a gate passed by an override and left out still makes it KARDINAL_OVERRIDDEN. A single gate too large for the record even cut (101 counted approvals and 101 counted Verdicts with long reasons) means the step starts without a record, and gets no receipt (status.receiptError), rather than waiting.

    A step that started before receipts were enabled has no record. Its receipt reads the gates at Verified and says so: decisionSnapshot: false, and kardinal verify-receipt prints "gates read when the step was verified".

    Size¶

    A receipt is at most 256 KiB. Past that, the approvals and Verdicts that did not count are dropped first (counted in approvalsOmitted and verdictsOmitted), then long gate texts are cut and the change requests' approvers dropped (approversOmitted), then the gates after the first and the metrics. truncated: true says so. The levels are those of the full record.

    How much receipts take: each PromotionReceipt has its size in status.bytes, each Pipeline environment its receipts' count and total size in status.environments[].receipts ({count, bytes}), and the controller counts the receipts it signs, kardinal_receipts_total{mode}, and their bytes, kardinal_receipt_bytes_total. Receipts past receipts.retention.maxAge are deleted; without it every receipt is kept, and kardinal doctor warns once they pass 1 GiB (they share etcd's quota, 8 GB by default, with every other object).

    Credentials are redacted¶

    A receipt is signed, and the Rekor sink publishes it for good: a signed record cannot be redacted afterwards without breaking its signature. So before the statement is signed, every string of kardinalPromotion and every subject name is redacted:

    • the user info of a URL: everything up to the last @ before the first / after ://, so a password with a raw #, ? or space goes too;
    • the value of a credential query or fragment parameter, up to the next &, ;, # or space, which becomes REDACTED. A credential parameter is one the Provenance check refuses: its normalized name ends in token, secret, key, signature, password, passphrase, session, ticket, bearer, ... (except harmless names such as monkey or sortkey), or is sig, auth, authorization, jwt, code, pass, pwd or sid. A percent-encoded name and an array name (token[]) count too;
    • a token known by its shape, wherever it is: GitHub (ghp_, gho_, ghu_, ghs_, ghr_, github_pat_), GitLab (glpat-) and Slack (xox?-) tokens, and AWS access key IDs (AKIA, ASIA).

    The JSON paths of the strings that changed are listed in kardinalPromotion.redacted, for example kardinalPromotion.version.provenance.ciRunURL. The Bundle is not changed.

    What is not detected: a credential of another shape outside a URL parameter, for example a bare password in provenance.author or in a gate's reason. Don't put credentials in a version's provenance or in gate texts.

    The Bundle API, kardinal create bundle and the Bundle CRD refuse a credential in ciRunURL in the first place. Redaction covers what gets past them: a version stored before the check, and text elsewhere in the record.

    Turn receipts on¶

    # values.yaml
receipts:
  enabled: true
  signing:
    mode: key            # keyless (default), key or kms
    key:
      secretRef:
        name: receipt-key

    cosign generate-key-pair      # or any ECDSA P-256 key, unencrypted PEM
kubectl -n kardinal-system create secret generic receipt-key --from-file=cosign.key=cosign.key

    kubectl apply the new CRD before you upgrade the chart, as for every release (Upgrading): promotionreceipts, and the PromotionStep's new status.decision status.pendingReceipt and status.receiptError fields.

    No promotion waits for a receipt, and no gate reads one. A receipt that cannot be signed or stored is retried with backoff (30 seconds, doubling, at most an hour) and counted in kardinal_receipts_failed_total{sink}. When Fulcio, Rekor or the timestamp authority answers 429 or 503 with Retry-After, the receipt waits the longer of that wait (at most 5 minutes) and its backoff, so a short Retry-After never makes it retry faster. Each wait gets a random part of up to the same length again, so the receipts of a burst do not all retry at once: a wait is between the backoff and twice it, at most two hours. status.lastAttemptAt and status.nextRetryAt show when the last failed attempt ran and when the next one runs. A receipt that can never be built (its statement is over the 256 KiB cap after every cut, or the version's Bundle or Pipeline is gone) does not hold the step either: the step is Verified without a PromotionReceipt, its status.receiptError says why, a ReceiptFailed AuditEvent records it, and kardinal_receipts_failed_total{sink="build"} counts it once.

    Signing modes¶

    Mode What a verifier pins When to use it
    keyless (default) The certificate identity https://kubernetes.io/namespaces/<ns>/serviceaccounts/<sa> and the cluster's OIDC issuer EKS, GKE and AKS, whose issuers the public-good Fulcio accepts. No key to keep. The controller gets a short-lived certificate from Fulcio with a projected ServiceAccount token of audience sigstore, separate from its API server token. kind and on-prem clusters need a private Fulcio that trusts their issuer (receipts.signing.keyless.fulcioURL). Keyless needs proof of the signing time: set receipts.sinks.rekor.url or receipts.signing.tsaURL.
    key The public key Air-gapped clusters. Anyone who can read the Secret can sign receipts; rotate it by replacing the Secret (the next receipt uses the new key).
    kms The public key Key custody outside the cluster: AWS KMS or Google Cloud KMS (receipts.signing.kmsKey, below). The private key never leaves the KMS.

    The keyless certificate names the ServiceAccount, not the cluster: always pin the issuer too.

    KMS keys¶

    The controller image is built with two KMS providers (Go build tags kms_aws and kms_gcp). A key reference for any other provider is refused at start, naming the provider and the ones built in. kardinal calls the provider itself and never sigstore's CLI-plugin fallback, which runs a subprocess.

    Provider receipts.signing.kmsKey Key Credentials
    AWS KMS awskms:///alias/kardinal-receipts, awskms:///arn:aws:kms:<region>:<account>:key/<id>, or with an endpoint, awskms://<host>:<port>/<id> Asymmetric, SIGN_VERIFY, ECC_NIST_P256 The SDK's default chain: IRSA (serviceAccount.annotations, eks.amazonaws.com/role-arn), EKS Pod Identity, or the node's instance role. The role needs kms:Sign, kms:GetPublicKey and kms:DescribeKey on the key (policy below). Prefer the key ARN to an alias: whoever may kms:UpdateAlias re-points an alias to another key. An endpoint that is a credential address (instance metadata, ECS, Pod Identity) is refused. The region comes from the ARN, or AWS_REGION.
    Google Cloud KMS gcpkms://projects/<p>/locations/<l>/keyRings/<r>/cryptoKeys/<k>/cryptoKeyVersions/<v>. The version is required: a reference without one is refused Asymmetric sign, EC_SIGN_P256_SHA256 Application Default Credentials: GKE Workload Identity, or GOOGLE_APPLICATION_CREDENTIALS. The identity needs four permissions on the key (below); roles/cloudkms.signerVerifier alone cannot sign.

    - Permissions. AWS: a policy on the controller's role, scoped to the key ARN (an alias reference still needs the key ARN as the resource; DescribeKey is required, as the signer reads the key's spec):

    {
  "Version": "2012-10-17",
  "Statement": [{
    "Effect": "Allow",
    "Action": ["kms:Sign", "kms:GetPublicKey", "kms:DescribeKey"],
    "Resource": "arn:aws:kms:<region>:<account>:key/<id>"
  }]
}

    Google Cloud: a custom role on the key with exactly cloudkms.cryptoKeys.get, cloudkms.cryptoKeyVersions.get, cloudkms.cryptoKeyVersions.useToSign and cloudkms.cryptoKeyVersions.viewPublicKey (the signer reads the key and its version before it signs), or the predefined roles/cloudkms.signer, roles/cloudkms.publicKeyViewer and roles/cloudkms.viewer together:

    gcloud iam roles create kardinalReceiptSigner --project <p> \
  --permissions cloudkms.cryptoKeys.get,cloudkms.cryptoKeyVersions.get,cloudkms.cryptoKeyVersions.useToSign,cloudkms.cryptoKeyVersions.viewPublicKey
gcloud kms keys add-iam-policy-binding <k> --keyring <r> --location <l> --project <p> \
  --member serviceAccount:<gsa> --role projects/<p>/roles/kardinalReceiptSigner
    - Egress. The KMS API calls (and AWS STS) go through the controller's egress guard and allowlist like every sink: with egress.allowlist set, add kms.<region>.amazonaws.com and sts.<region>.amazonaws.com, or cloudkms.googleapis.com. The credential requests to the instance metadata, ECS and Pod Identity endpoints (and Google's metadata server) are the cloud SDK's own, not user input, and are not guarded. - Rotation. A receipt records the key reference it was signed with (status.signedBy); a verifier pins the public key. A Cloud KMS reference must pin its key version: without one the provider would sign with the newest enabled version, so whoever may add a version would change the signing key. Rotating is a configuration change: set the new version's reference and give verifiers its public key. The key is loaded once, at the first signature, and its client is reused until the controller stops. A key disabled in the KMS fails signing (Signed=False, SignFailed), retried with backoff and counted in kardinal_receipts_failed_total{sink="signing"}; promotions are not held. - Timeouts. Each KMS call is bounded by the signer's timeout (30 s); an endpoint the egress guard refuses fails at once, naming the address.

    Where receipts are stored¶

    Sink Values Notes
    The PromotionReceipt (always) — status.bundle holds the signed receipt (a Sigstore bundle). Lives and dies with the cluster.
    OCI referrer receipts.sinks.oci.enabled, receipts.sinks.oci.secretRef.name Pushed as a referrer of each image, artifact type application/vnd.dev.sigstore.bundle.v0.3+json, annotated dev.sigstore.bundle.predicateType: https://slsa.dev/verification_summary/v1, kardinal.io/receipt: "true" (a reader of attestations tells a receipt from a builder's own VSA by it; verify-receipt reads only those) and kardinal.io/receipt-resource (its resourceUri, so verify-receipt --image reads only the environment it is asked about). Needs push rights: a kubernetes.io/dockerconfigjson Secret in the release namespace, never a tenant's pull Secret. A config version has no image and is not pushed.
    Rekor receipts.sinks.rekor.url, receipts.sinks.rekor.version (1 or 2) Every signature is logged: in Rekor v1 (rekor.sigstore.dev), or with version 2 in a Rekor v2 (rekor-tiles) log, such as the public log20xx shards or sigstore scaffolding's, where a receipt is a hashedrekord entry. A Rekor v2 entry proves inclusion but not the signing time, so keyless signing with version 2 needs receipts.signing.tsaURL too: the chart and the controller refuse it without one (a receipt that could never verify once its short-lived certificate expired is never marked Signed). A key or KMS receipt in Rekor v2 needs no timestamp authority. The public-good Rekor (rekor.sigstore.dev) publishes each receipt's names, emails and pipeline names, so it needs receipts.sinks.rekor.public: true as well. A private Rekor is the enterprise path.

    The OCI sink pushes with the controller's Secret, but a Bundle's image repositories are chosen by whoever creates the Bundle. In a shared install, bound where it writes: set receipts.sinks.oci.repository (every referrer goes to one repository you own), or receipts.sinks.oci.allowedRepositories (a registry, or a registry and path prefix: ghcr.io/acme/, docker.io/acme). An image's repository is parsed as the registry sees it, and matched by registry and whole path components: docker.io/acme and acme are one, a repository with a ., .. or empty path component (ghcr.io/acme/../other) is never allowed. An image outside the list gets no referrer: its receipt is still signed and kept, and its Stored condition is False with reason RepositoryNotAllowed, naming the repository. Left empty, the sink pushes to every image's repository its Secret reaches.

    Both values are the controller's, not a namespace's. If one tenant's Bundle names an image in a repository the list allows for another tenant, the referrer is pushed there: a write into the other tenant's repository. verify-receipt pins the namespace, so it does not take that referrer as the other tenant's receipt. Where tenants must not write into each other's repositories, run one release per tenant, each with its own list (One install per team namespace). Shards with a list each separate the pushes too, but shards are not a trust boundary (Sharding). A per-namespace list is planned (#2285).

    ImageVerification skips receipt referrers before it counts an image's signatures, so an image promoted to many environments does not crowd out its own signature (Image verification).

    The PromotionReceipt¶

    $ kubectl get promotionreceipts -n payments
NAME                  PIPELINE   ENV    BUNDLE           PHASE    AGE
checkout-1-5-0-prod   checkout   prod   checkout-1-5-0   Stored   2m
    • Name: <bundle>-<environment>-<hash>, the hash of the Bundle, the environment and the step's UID, so no two promotions share one; the step records it in status.receipt, and the receipt is labelled with the step's UID (kardinal.io/promotion-step-uid). Re-running the Verified transition finds it; a receipt of that name that is not the step's is never adopted (status.receiptError).
    • Phase: Pending (not signed yet), Signed (signed; a sink is not written yet), Stored (every sink holds it), Failed (its statement does not match spec.statementDigest: it was changed, and is never signed).
    • Conditions: Signed, Stored and PRCommented, with reasons naming a failing sink.
    • Signed once: a status write that meets a conflict after signing re-reads the receipt and records the same signature, rather than signing again (a second certificate and, with Rekor, a second log entry). A controller that crashes between signing and its status write signs again on restart: both signatures verify.
    • No owner: a receipt outlives its Bundle, the Graph and the step. Deleting a Bundle does not delete its receipts. kardinal keeps every receipt unless you set receipts.retention.maxAge (for example 8760h): the leader then deletes the receipts created longer ago, at most 2,000 an hour. The sinks outside the cluster keep their copy whatever it says.
    • Size: a receipt is typically a few KB. Its statement is capped at 256 KiB, and the signed bundle repeats it in base64, so one receipt is at most about 600 KiB in etcd: 10,000 receipts of that worst case would be about 6 GB, against etcd's default 8 GB quota. If your gates carry very large expressions or approval reasons, set receipts.retention.maxAge and keep the long-term copy in the OCI or Rekor sink. The controller caches a receipt it is done with (stored and commented, or failed) without its statement and bundle, so its memory does not grow with them.
    • Writes: spec is immutable and status.bundle cannot change once set (the CRD refuses both). The <release>-receipts admission policy refuses every create, update and delete of a PromotionReceipt by anyone but the controller, a cluster-admin included; the namespace controller and the garbage collector may delete them. User roles can read them.

    Verify a receipt¶

    # From the cluster: the newest receipt of the environment, or --bundle
kardinal verify-receipt checkout prod --key receipt.pub
kardinal verify-receipt checkout prod --bundle checkout-1-5-0 \
  --identity https://kubernetes.io/namespaces/kardinal-system/serviceaccounts/kardinal-promoter \
  --issuer https://oidc.eks.us-east-2.amazonaws.com/id/EXAMPLE

# From the image's OCI referrers, in any cluster or none: pin the namespace, the Pipeline,
# the environment and the cluster (receipts.clusterID, or its kube-system Namespace UID)
CLUSTER=$(kubectl get namespace kube-system -o jsonpath='{.metadata.uid}')
kardinal verify-receipt --image ghcr.io/acme/checkout@sha256:9f2c... \
  -n payments --pipeline checkout --env prod --cluster "$CLUSTER" --key receipt.pub

# From a file: pin the subject too
kubectl get promotionstep <step> -o jsonpath='{.status.receipt}'   # the receipt's name
kubectl get promotionreceipt <receipt> -o jsonpath='{.status.bundle}' > receipt.sigstore.json
kardinal verify-receipt --file receipt.sigstore.json -n payments --pipeline checkout --env prod \
  --cluster "$CLUSTER" --subject sha256:9f2c... --key receipt.pub \
  --require GATES_PASSED,APPROVED,!OVERRIDDEN

    It checks, and exits non-zero when any check fails:

    1. The signature: with --key, or for a keyless receipt the certificate chain against the trusted root (--trusted-root, default the public-good root) and the exact --identity and --issuer. Both are required: no default trusts any identity.
    2. The log entry or timestamp, when the receipt has one, against the trusted root. It never contacts Rekor: the entry and its inclusion proof are in the receipt. A key receipt verified without --trusted-root does not check its log entry, and says so ("entry not checked"; logged: false, logNotChecked: true with -o json): it is in the transparency log only once the entry is verified (✓ transparency log: inclusion verified (logIndex N, log ...)).
    3. What it is for: the cluster, namespace, Pipeline and environment of its resourceUri, exactly, and the subject. From the cluster these are the receipt's own; with --image and --file, -n, --pipeline and --env are required, --subject too for a file (the --image digest is the subject), and --cluster whenever the receipt names a cluster (its kube-system Namespace UID, or receipts.clusterID); --any-cluster accepts any cluster's, with a warning. A receipt of another tenant's Pipeline, or another cluster's, signed with the same key for the same image, is refused.
    4. The summary: PASSED, with each --required level present (!LEVEL absent). A SLSA level is the highest of its track, as a verification summary lists it: SLSA_BUILD_LEVEL_3 satisfies --require SLSA_BUILD_LEVEL_2, and !SLSA_BUILD_LEVEL_3 means a build level below 3.
    5. From the cluster: the PromotionReceipt's statement is the signed one, byte for byte, and the Bundle it names, when it still exists, has the UID it records. A receipt edited after it was signed fails.

    Each check is a line of its own, ✓ when it was made and passed, – when it was not made (a subject not pinned, a log entry not checked, no level required): the output never says just "verified". -o json prints the same list (checks, each with name, checked and detail) and the statement.

    With --image, verify-receipt reads the image's referrers, following the registry's pages (the Referrers API's Link: rel="next", on the same registry only; or the referrers tag schema), up to 1024 of them. Each receipt referrer carries its resourceUri as an annotation (kardinal.io/receipt-resource, not signed: it only narrows the search, and the receipt read is verified), so another environment's receipts are skipped before they are read and cannot crowd out the one asked for. An image with more referrers than are read gets a warning, and referrersTruncated: true with -o json; if no receipt of the pinned resource is among those read, or none of those read verifies (receipts planted by whoever may push to the repository, say, up to the 64 that are read), verify-receipt fails with "cannot verify": the real one may be among those not read, so whether one exists is unknown, which is never a pass. A registry whose list cannot be read whole fails it too ("cannot verify"): more than 64 pages, a page linked twice, or a next page on another host or scheme. The whole command gives up after --timeout (60 s by default), also with "cannot verify": reading the cluster, the registry and its credentials (a docker credential helper that does not answer is cut off), and the public-good trusted root's TUF fetch.

    Where receipts show¶

    • The PR: when a receipt is signed, the controller comments once on the promotion's PR: "Promotion receipt: checkout-1-5-0-prod, signed by …, stored in …. Verify: kardinal verify-receipt checkout prod --bundle checkout-1-5-0". The receipt records the PR too, so the two point at each other. A comment that keeps failing is given up after 10 attempts (condition PRCommented=False, reason GaveUp). The comment ends with a hidden marker, <!-- kardinal-receipt:<namespace>/<receipt> -->: the controller searches the PR's comments for it before posting (GitHub, GitLab, Forgejo/Gitea, Bitbucket Cloud and Data Center, Azure DevOps), so a post whose status write was lost (a crash) is not posted again. Only a comment by kardinal's own SCM identity counts (the token's user; a GitHub App's comments by the App), so anyone who may comment on the PR cannot keep the receipt's comment from being posted by posting the marker first. The search reads at most 20 pages of comments, oldest first. A token that may not read its own user (a Forgejo/Gitea token with only the write:repository and write:issue scopes, a Bitbucket token without account) is not searched: the comment is posted, and a crash between the post and its status write can post it twice. Add read:user (Forgejo/Gitea) or account (Bitbucket) to search.
    • kardinal history adds a RECEIPT column once a promotion has one: "signed" once the controller signed it. "Verified" is only what kardinal verify-receipt says of a receipt it checked.
    • kardinal why names the receipt of the version an environment runs, with the command to verify it.
    • The UI: the stage drawer's History says "Receipt : signed ✓" with the command to verify it once the step's receipt is signed.

    Environments with approval: auto have no PR: their receipts show in history, why and the UI.

    What a receipt does not prove¶

    • The controller signs what it decides. A receipt is as true as the controller. An admin who can mint the controller's ServiceAccount tokens, or read the key Secret, can sign anything. The signature makes the record tamper-evident once it leaves the cluster, and says who stands behind it.
    • Asserted fields stay asserted. The model and session in the actor are what the client said about itself.
    • Nothing after Verified. A revert outside kardinal, or drift, is not in the receipt.
    • A deleted PromotionReceipt with no other sink is gone. Use the OCI or Rekor sink for evidence you must keep.
